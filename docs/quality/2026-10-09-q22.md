## Taxonomy corrections

Natural phenomenon 1651 was incorrectly confined to physical process.
Its genus is now phenomenon 2535. That broader category admits natural
conditions as well as processes. Weather 2074 is an atmospheric phenomenon
and a state. Its exclusion from natural phenomenon, edge 3478, is rejected.

Rain and snowfall now belong to the falling-precipitation family, which
also has physical-process ancestry. Wind retains explicit physical-process
ancestry. Fog remains an atmospheric phenomenon; its material aerosol sense
is not combined with that record. Climate retains the longer-term pattern
meaning without a mandatory averaging interval. Other natural phenomena
retain their natural-phenomenon genera; their more specific physical
classification is left for individual review rather than inherited from
the former overbroad root assertion.

The U1 exclusion scan falls from 15 to **14 candidate pairs**, with no
new conflict or witness. The scan covers accepted, nonzero U1 genus paths
and codes 61, 63 and 64, not all possible semantic contradictions.

## New coverage and boundaries

Precipitation processes distinguish short rain and snow episodes, falling
hail, freezing rain and drizzle, and mixed rain/snow. Hail material remains
separate from hailfall. The mixed episode has rain and snowfall components;
neither component is asserted to be its sole genus. The bare English word
*sleet* is omitted because regional meanings differ.

Rainy and windy weather have an explicit shared subtype. A whole weather
condition is not made a subtype of its rain or wind component. Air
temperature, cloud fraction, visibility distance, snow depth and
water-equivalent precipitation depth remain quantities. Snow depth has no
assumed constant conversion to liquid-water equivalent.

Measuring actions and instrument purposes connect the new quantities to
their use. Rain gauges are distinguished from the broader precipitation
instrument family; no heater, fixed precision or all-phase capability is
required. Existing pressure, humidity, dew point, wind speed, thermometers,
barometers, hygrometers and anemometers are reused.

Forecast information is separate from forecasting activity. Weather
forecasting produces a forecast, and observing-and-recording produces an
observation record. Neither activity is asserted to produce the weather.
