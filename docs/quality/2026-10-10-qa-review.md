# QA generator: adequacy review

The local generator produces questions with computed answers and the facts
needed to derive those answers. The review uses Q40, everyday context 1, seed
42, and generator version 1. See the [usage and format guide](../qa-generator.md).

## Pilot results

| Check | English | Russian |
|---|---:|---:|
| Requested / generated | 200 / 200 | 200 / 200 |
| Passed source and answer checks | 200 | 200 |
| Duplicate questions | 0 | 0 |
| Semantically inspected examples | 27 | 27 |
| Nonempty / empty intersections | 12 / 8 | 10 / 10 |
| Established / unsupported ancestry answers | 13 / 7 | 10 / 10 |
| Positive / negative / unknown property answers | 20 / 20 / 20 | 20 / 20 / 20 |
| Conflicting property answers in this real-data sample | 0 | 0 |

Each language contains 20 examples from each of seven non-property task
families and 60 property examples. A synthetic fixture tests conflicting
inheritance separately; no synthetic conflict is presented as a real graph fact.
The [machine-readable review](2026-10-10-qa-review.json) records distributions,
rejections, reviewed example IDs, graph and policy hashes, and export hashes.

## What was checked

Every generated record is solved independently from its supplied facts, then
checked against the source graph. Relevant tasks also replay their expression
through the concept algebra evaluator. Checks cover source labels and edges,
complete evidence for the task, finite domains, overrides, negative assertions,
wording/answer consistency and duplicates. Missing suitable data yields an
explicit shortfall rather than repeated or invented examples.

The semantic inspection covers three examples from each non-property family
and two examples from each available property state, in both languages. It
examines the wording, answer and full supplied facts. These 54 examples were
first inspected on Q39; after lexical cleanup, their questions, answers,
expected values, facts and chat messages were compared exactly with the final
Q40 exports. Only source identity and the resulting record IDs changed.

This is an editorial review by the coding assistant, not independent human
annotation. It is a sample review, not a correctness claim for every possible
generated example or every fact in the database.

## Problems found and changes made

- The initial intersection sample was almost entirely empty. Candidate
  selection now mixes witnessed and empty intersections.
- Count questions initially made the answer equal to the supplied domain
  size. They now include irrelevant domain records and deduplicate overlap.
- Ancestry questions initially offered only established paths; inference
  questions were dominated by unsupported conclusions. Generation now includes
  reverse-direction ancestry checks and supported common-membership inference.
- Short English aliases could read as verbs or adjectives where the question
  needed a noun. Reviewed preferences select `disease`, `cognition` and
  `clarification`; quoted terms and concept IDs keep senses explicit.
- Legacy Russian forms could expose artificial nouns to training. The
  [Q40 cleanup](2026-10-10-q40.md) removes demonstrated errors. Generation also
  requires a usable Latin-script English term in either output language,
  excluding the untranslated legacy branch. That is a coverage filter,
  not proof that every retained word is correct.
- Source assertions can be wrong even when the inference is mechanically
  valid. The [QA policy](../../concept_algebra/qa_policy.json) excludes known
  problematic concepts and assertions, including inherited evidence.

For example, a general denial of flight for ants overlooks reproductive
winged ants. The [Natural History Museum](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/when-why-winged-ants-swarm-nuptial-flight.html)
describes that life stage. A stored genus link classifies lentils as cereal
plants; [Kew](https://www.kew.org/plants/lentil) identifies the legume family.
Those examples are excluded from generated training targets. The generator
does not silently edit the underlying assertions; relation repairs remain
separate reviewed work.

## Limits for training

Answers are conditional on supplied graph facts. A negative answer can mean
an explicitly negative assertion or lack of entailment, depending on the task;
templates distinguish those cases. Empty finite catalog intersections never
establish real-world disjointness.

Keep complete prompts when training: omitting the supplied facts changes the
task. Pin the graph and generator versions. Keep each `group_id` within one
split and check shared concepts and evidence across groups; grouping alone
does not remove every leakage path. The pilot does not measure learning gains
in an LLM, and no model was trained during this review.

The local implementation passes 67 algebra/QA Python tests, five lexical
regressions, Go tests, nine JavaScript collection checks, and 17 browser scenarios
documented in the [visualizer guide](../../visualizer/README.md#browser-checks).
