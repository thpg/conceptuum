## Corrected meanings

The existing broadleaf-tree record 249 had the English label *deciduous
tree*. Leaf form and seasonal retention are different classifications.
That label now belongs to deciduous tree 24882; record 249
uses *broadleaf tree* and *broad-leaved tree*. Larch 5578 now belongs to
deciduous conifers, and true fir 5577 to evergreen conifers. Both retain
tree ancestry. Oak is not assigned a universal deciduous parent.

Forest/grove exclusion 9483 and forest/taiga exclusion 9482 contradicted
their accepted genera. Those exclusion rows are rejected as reviewed
history; the genus rows are preserved. Forest/jungle exclusion 3723 is also
rejected because the forest meaning of jungle supplies a counterexample.
The wider jungle/thicket scope is not forced beneath forest.

The U1 exclusion scan falls from 17 to **15 candidate pairs**,
with no new pair or witness. It covers accepted, nonzero U1 genus edges and
codes 61, 63 and 64; it is not a complete semantic-consistency proof.

## Added coverage

Tree families distinguish evergreen and deciduous foliage from the
conifer/broadleaf classification. Forest families separate dominant tree
type, foliage retention, rainfall and climatic region. Temperate rainforest
and deciduous conifer forest have both relevant genera, supporting Euler
intersections without invented overlap percentages.

A mixed coniferous/broadleaf forest has both kinds of tree as components;
that does not make it a member of both dominance classes. Taiga is not
forced beneath evergreen coniferous forest. Forest as a wooded area is
separate from forest vegetation as collective plant life. The shrub
understorey entry restricts the broader English undergrowth meaning to
the selected Russian forestry sense.
