## Corrected structure and vocabulary

Existing card-game activity 5595 is now explicitly named as an activity.
Its English term `playing cards` moves to the new physical-card concept;
`card game` names the existing activity. The physical card retains the
existing card-artifact genus 2998. Existing dominoes denotes a game, while
new domino tiles denote its physical playing pieces.

The existing ball record is now game equipment. A separate toy-ball family
retains the valid overlap with toys, while sporting balls need not be toys.
The assertion that a ball itself performs play is rejected; its existing
play-purpose relation is preserved. Basketball and volleyball activities
are distinct from their physical balls.

Football, hockey, basketball, volleyball and baseball gain the nearer team
sport genus. Tennis gains the racket-sport genus. Existing chess, checkers
and dominoes gain suitable game families while retaining previous ancestry.
Hockey is not universally asserted to use a ball or an ice surface.

The former sport/play exclusion is rejected before shared game/sport
subtypes are added. The gymnastics/sport exclusion is rejected while the
sporting genus is retained. The existing gymnastics record is qualified
as the sport; broader gymnastic exercises are represented separately.

Social action and physical action describe compatible aspects of an
activity. Their blanket exclusion is rejected; sporting gymnastics is a
reviewed shared subtype. This does not validate every old action descendant
or classify every sport as physical exercise: the sport branch also retains
chess. The U1 exclusion scan falls from 12 to **10 conflicting pairs**,
with no new pair or witness among the remaining exclusions. The scan covers
accepted, nonzero U1 genus paths and codes 61, 63 and 64, not every semantic
contradiction. The ten remaining pairs are listed in the [active plan](../../PLAN.md).

## Added coverage and intersections

Tennis, table tennis and squash have both racket-sport and ball-sport
classifications. Badminton uses a shuttlecock and is not made a ball sport.
Doubles tennis, badminton and table tennis also belong to the selected
team-format family. Singles formats are not treated as incompatible with
participation in a wider team competition.

The physical racket family allows a solid table-tennis striking surface;
strings are not made universal. Shuttlecocks allow feathered and synthetic
construction. Balls, rackets, cards, tiles and boards have purpose relations
to their activities, not activity genera or agent capabilities.

Board, card and tile game families classify game mechanics. Their inclusion
does not require every game occurrence to use physical equipment: digital
representations remain possible. Equipment records explicitly denote physical
artifacts. A rigid board is not required of every card or domino game.
